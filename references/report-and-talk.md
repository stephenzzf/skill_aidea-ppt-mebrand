# Report and talk planning in standard ME2026

## Two management modes

Record the audience's task, review setting, approved content permissions and evidence boundary. Use the same ME2026 visual system and editable PowerPoint primitives for both modes.

| Decision | Detailed management report | Management decision talk |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Audience task | Read independently, look up comparable rows, discuss or approve individual items. | Follow one primary judgment per slide at projection size, then respond to the speaker. |
| Page hierarchy | Use a compact heading and aligned body or a matrix. Place evidence, qualifications and actions beside the corresponding judgment. Repeat table headers and row keys on continuations. Use a native decision table with item, proposal and responsibility when those fields are supplied. | Give the first visual emphasis to the principal outcome, distinction or next decision. Use a dominant metric, visual or native shape where it advances that point. Keep related blocks in a clear reading sequence. Add a specific cue in notes for what to point to, ask or hand off. |
| Density and type | Start around 16 pt for inline body, 12 pt minimum for tables and chart labels, and 10 pt for source notes. Allocate table widths according to actual cell text and review the last row and longest label at final size. | Start at 20–22 pt for primary prose and follow the [talk hierarchy](brand-guidelines.md) for headings and tables. Use consistent anchors, grouping and spacing. Mark a slide for reading time when a source-locked table cannot be scanned at speaking pace. |
| Main and supporting visuals | A table or chart earns the primary area when the reader needs to compare values, scope or responsibilities. Keep an explanatory diagram close to its accompanying paragraph. Use a scene as an inset if it adds context. Omit optional illustrations that would crowd the evidence. | Put a diagram or scene in the primary area when the speaker needs to point to a specific relationship or an action. Captions and editable labels clarify what to follow. Keep the relevant values, units, conditions and decision owners on the slide. |
| Notes | Put self-contained explanations on the page when needed to understand the claim. Notes may carry supplementary method or sources as authorized. | Give spoken cues that refer to actual slide objects. Keep required copy and key qualifications visible even when the speaker mentions them. |

For a source-locked deck, preserve the agreed wording, order, pagination and image scope in both modes. Use type weight, width, native grouping and position for emphasis. Reconcile ordered text in the editable objects and check the rendered page as a reader would encounter it. Record layout-driven breaks without changing wording or units. Treat a displayed number, its approximation marker and its unit as one visual phrase. If bold or enlarged runs split the phrase across lines, move the complete phrase to the next line, widen its frame or change which parts receive emphasis. Put the condition next to the resulting figure. Keep automatic table pagination off for fixed page counts, reserve the existing table-to-content gap, and inspect tables for clipped rows, isolated punctuation or unintended single-character wraps. Use `autoShrink: false` where the type size is intentional. Request a revised plan only if resolution requires changing the agreed scope.

Choose the medium via the [shared visual rules](intake-and-confirmation.md#page-visuals). Keep business terms, amounts and meaningful relationships editable. Use code for a layout-sensitive concept interface, a branch or a return path when native shapes alone would be hard to read. Use an action-focused image if people or objects teach the point. Show a traceable original record for claims about actual events. Evaluate the visual’s role as well as its format. Reusing the same picture in two modes can be appropriate when its size, placement and reading role support each audience. When moving an illustration to a different claim or page, name the action it actually shows and the specific step it illustrates. Use a scoped caption such as “实施验收示意” if the picture shows sign-off while the page covers a wider delivery model. If readers could infer an unsupported outcome or attribute the pictured action to the whole process, narrow the caption and positioning or omit the image.

For example, a two-route investment proposal can use a native table first in the report, pairing each route with outcomes, investment and risks. In a talk the audience should be able to trace the two routes at a glance before comparing the same fields while the speaker points to them. In a study example, put population, dates and exclusions where the reader can see them beside the relevant figure in both modes. Use an inset physical scene to explain the activity only if the data stays easy to find.

### Reading task and adjacent-page review

In the page plan, record the question a reader should answer, the first object they should see and the qualification they need to interpret it. Choose a layout after locating those elements in the source.

| Reading task | Composition | Check on the rendered slide |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Find a value or review a register | Native table with stable row keys and repeated headings. | Locate the requested cell and its unit. Keep continuation pages aligned. |
| Explain what drives a result | A comparable series in a native chart, or a highlighted intersection when the full operating table is needed. | Identify the driver and find the period, basis and exceptions before comparing figures. Keep unavailable values distinct from zero. |
| Follow a calculation | Separate assumptions, calculation and result. Give alternative time horizons a small comparison table. | Follow the units between steps. Distinguish percent growth from percentage-point contribution, and forecast from observation. |
| Distinguish reporting scopes | Two aligned panels with the same questions about inclusion, exclusion and interpretation. | Tell which result can support the decision and identify what still requires a separate account. |
| Decide or approve items | Use one row per issue. Put the decision to be taken in a consistent column beside the proposal and the responsible party if supplied. | Identify the open question without mistaking a recommendation for approval or assigning an unsupported owner. |

Review each changed page alongside its predecessor and successor at presentation size. Name the first visible object and use it to answer the planned question. When adjacent slides repeat a layout, keep it if the reader is continuing the same lookup; when the reading task changes, adjust the hierarchy or composition so the new question is apparent. A new icon or fill alone does not resolve a mismatch. Recheck the detail table, data conditions and brand anchors after the change.

### Reference deck review

Match by reader question, including split appendices and extra reference pages. Review three things on the rendered slide:

- **Layout.** Trace the first object to its interpretation. A two-path concept diagram can explain where an interaction occurs; an adjacent native chart can explain the direction or size of a measured difference. Verify the sign, date and reference population from the brief before borrowing a reference treatment.
- **Density.** Test one specific lookup at normal size. For a locked appendix, group stable IDs by reader question, keep each record identifiable, label pending or forecast values in text, and carry full fields in notes. Link only to URLs supplied for that record. If records have to be opened side by side, document the advantage of a multi-page reference.
- **Imagery.** For a magnitude comparison, use a common-scale chart for the comparable series; separate independent studies with their own source and sample. For a level or stage, a single sourced example carried through every level can teach more than an icon per step. Use photos for physical actions and traceable originals for evidence.

Prioritize a wrong reading, an invisible comparison or a missing lookup before cosmetic differences. For each selected page record one reader task, its original obstacle, the change and what remains harder. Check both branches of a signed chart, the first and last entries of an index, and the provenance of any worked example. [Editable analytical layouts](analytical-layouts.md)

### Acceptance by use

- **Report:** Review at normal screen size. Locate a qualification without looking elsewhere, compare the same field across routes, find who owns the decision and verify continuation headers. Look for the next section, note or figure with a visible gap after the last table row.
- **Talk:** Review at the planned projection size. Name the first outcome you noticed, follow the picture's path and point to the object named in the notes. Check that labels and qualifications remain readable, and identify the slides where fixed copy merits a pause.
- **Paired test:** Use the same source, permissions and materials, changing the use only. Compare aligned topics for visual hierarchy, where the qualification lives, and whether the visual helps someone complete the intended task. Record actual rendering results and any scope-driven tradeoffs. Counting words, objects or pictures alone does not demonstrate the intended style.

For a team keynote, emphasize narrative progression and handoffs. For a product demo, structure the page around the observed input, action and result. Both keep the same evidence and editability standards.

## Learning from a reference talk

Compare matched topics at presentation size. Take the color roles, alignment, focal object and order in which the eye reaches the evidence from specific reference pages. Note what the speaker can actually point to. In the candidate, use the same accent for the same job across slides, such as a decision, a sequence or a qualified metric. Choose the composition from each page’s underlying structure: progression, fork, handoff, responsibility, set of alternatives or a vote. Keep all figures paired with their units, populations and conditions. A reference headline that synthesizes or changes the brief needs explicit editing permission. In notes, name the specific object to point to and the question or transition it supports. Check that the referenced column, branch or number really exists where the cue says it does.

## Composition recipes

- **Judgment and evidence.** Use an editable headline, native chart or matched metric boxes, adjacent qualification and readable source line. Keep dates, denominators, samples, units and forecast status with the numbers. Prefer separate charts for different measures. A native chart should include the underlying workbook and match its labels and numbers.
- **Phase and deliverable.** Draw directional connectors only for sequence. Put parallel evaluation criteria in a separate row. Align each phase with its owner, decision, artifact or acceptance test. Use native shapes and labels so changing a name or step does not require editing a picture.
- **Two workflows.** Use `addME2026DualPath` from `scripts/scene_components.cjs` with two labeled lanes, 2–4 short steps in each, and a distinct outcome and detail for each lane. Call on a standard white base; review text fit in the rendered slide. If the lanes share a feedback step, put that in a separate text zone.
- **Case and boundary.** Describe what happened, who was responsible, where the evidence came from, what was observed, and what cannot be inferred. Distinguish historical facts, retrospective interpretations and suggested methods. Treat absent baselines or unverified attribution as visible limitations. Evidence labels such as E1–E4 are optional.
- **Product fit.** Use an editable table with product, fit, non-fit, integration and evaluation. Keep delivery or service activities distinct from native product functions. Preserve the scope qualifiers in the visible table or immediately adjacent text.

## Sources, page references and demo materials

Assign stable source IDs. In the plan or speaker notes bind each claim to title, publisher, date/version, URL or internal locator, and verification status with basis. Display the material qualification beside the claim. In the finished deck use a native hyperlink where a public URL is verified and a textual locator where an internal reference has no appropriate public link. Check that each in-deck reference points to the final page and that appendix “used on” values match the visible claim pages. A marker inherited from a source deck is not verification.

For the talk, plan the visual subject first. Choose a product interface, business diagram, evidence panel or native flow according to the slide’s claim. When an approved plan calls for an interface, a text-only slide cannot fulfill it. Use [code visuals](code-visuals.md) for concept interfaces with concrete content, and use a standard editable slide around the image. For a demo, specify what happens at each step, the time budget, expected outcome and static fallback. An embedded video requires a recorded insertion location and actual playback verification when playback is part of the approved plan. Label pending media and static alternatives explicitly. Use screenshots as evidence only where provenance and permission are clear. For an explanatory illustration, verify its relationship to the page claim and to the editable diagram. Apply [native imagery](native-imagery.md) and [QA](qa-and-content-contract.md).
